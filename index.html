The path of discovery

Go in order. Each experiment answers a question that the one before it left open.

  1. 1928 · Experiment 1

    Griffith’s transformation experiment

    Frederick Griffith

    Can something pass from dead bacteria to living ones and change their traits?

    Inject mice with live and heat-killed bacteria, wait five days, and test the blood.

  2. 1944 · Experiment 2

    The Avery–MacLeod–McCarty experiment

    Oswald Avery, Colin MacLeod, and Maclyn McCarty

    Which molecule is the “transforming principle”?

    Purify an extract from bacteria, analyze it, and use enzymes to destroy one type of molecule at a time.

    Open simulation ↗Open lab sheet ↗
  3. 1952 · Experiment 3

    The Hershey–Chase experiment

    Alfred Hershey and Martha Chase

    When a virus infects a cell, does it inject protein or DNA?

    Tag a phage’s protein and DNA with radioactive labels, then blend, centrifuge, and measure where each label ends up.

  4. 1951–1953 · Experiment 4

    Franklin’s X-ray diffraction experiment

    Rosalind Franklin and Raymond Gosling

    What shape is the DNA molecule?

    Photograph DNA fibers with X-rays, measure the pattern on the film, and build a model that reproduces it.

    Open simulation ↗Open lab sheet ↗
  5. 1958 · Experiment 5

    The Meselson–Stahl experiment

    Matthew Meselson and Franklin Stahl

    How does a cell copy its DNA?

    Label DNA with heavy nitrogen, let bacteria copy it, separate it by density, and compare three models of replication.
